How to use Selah, page by page. Every heading folds: click the arrow beside it. Press Ctrl+K anywhere to jump to a page or find a note.

This guide opens read-only. Selah updates it when a new version comes out, so changes you make here would be replaced. To keep your own notes on it, use Duplicate in the ⋯ menu. Earlier versions are in Version history.

Getting around

The rail

  • The rail down the left, in groups: your day (Home, with what's active on Desk as its tabs; Plan, the calendar and your tasks; and the Inbox), then Walk (Daily Walk, Reading Plans and Prayer, a tab each), Bible, Desk (all your studies, lessons, sermons, writing and classes) and Preach, then your WordPress Site (while one is connected) and My notes. A count shows what's waiting in the Inbox and the tasks due. Selah always opens on Home; reloading keeps the page you're on.

  • My notes is every note you have (but what's still in the Inbox), sorted by tags: the row of tags under the search box shows one tag's notes, Files shows notes with attachments, and All goes back. Builders' notes are there too, tagged with their work (a sermon series, a study, a class). Drop a note from the Inbox on My notes to file it without a tag.

  • There are no notebooks or shelves to manage. The builders keep their notes together behind the scenes, and everything else lives in My notes with its tags.

  • Utility sits near the foot of the rail, under New: your templates, recent attachments and tags, each with a link to its whole page, what's shared with you, and the Trash.

  • At the foot: New (the round button: a note, or one from a template), then Utility and Settings. Esc or a click outside closes Utility. On a short screen the whole rail scrolls.

  • Focus mode and Refresh are the two buttons at the right of the top bar, beside the search box.

  • Tabs run along the top of every page and work like a web browser's. The first tab, the main tab (with a house), follows you around Selah: the rail, links and Back all open things there. To keep a note, study or course open beside it, Ctrl-click it, middle-click it, or right-click → Open in new tab; what you open from inside that tab then opens in it. Pages work the same way: Ctrl-click, middle-click or right-click a page on the rail, or Templates, Attachments, Tags, Media or the Trash in Utility. A builder always opens in a tab of its own: clicking the rail from a builder takes you back to the main tab and leaves the builder open, and closing the builder closes its tab. Right-click a tab to Pin it, or right-click the main tab → Pin this as a tab, or a rail page → Pin as a tab. Pinned tabs come first, stay narrow, have no close button and keep what they hold: a note or study you open from a pinned tab opens in the main tab. Also or Close others, Close to the right or Close all but pinned. A middle click closes a tab. When there are many, the tabs get narrower.

  • The tour: a two-minute look at what's where (the rail, Home and Desk, tabs, the passage page, preaching and slides), with Next, Back and Skip. It's offered once; play it again from Settings → About → Take the tour. It can make a sample word study to look around in, which one click deletes (from the tour, or Settings → About).

  • Back and Forward: the arrows at the left of the tabs, Alt+← and Alt+→, or your mouse's side buttons step through the places you've been: pages, notes, studies on Desk and passages on the Bible page.

  • On a phone a bar runs along the bottom: your first two pages, New, My notes and More (everything else, Utility and Settings too, as a sheet). Menus (a note's ⋯, or a long press) open as a sheet along the bottom of the screen.

Make it yours: Settings → General → Customize the rail…, or right-click the rail (or Ctrl+K → "Customize sidebar").

  • Untick a page to take it off the rail; right-click the rail to reach it (it's listed above Customize), or Ctrl+K. On a phone it's under More. My notes and Utility can come off too.

  • Drag the pages, or use the arrows, to put them in your order. Reset order puts them back.

  • Your choices follow you to your other devices.

Search or jump: Ctrl+K

Press Ctrl+K (or click Search at the top right).

  • Type part of a page, tag, study or note title. Notes are also found by their text.

  • Your recent notes show before you type.

  • Type a Bible reference to read it on the Scripture page. New study… starts a Bible study.

  • Run actions: new note, new task, new event, quick capture, voice memo, settings and theme.

  • ↑ and ↓ move, Enter opens.

Right-click menus

Most things have a right-click menu (a long press on Android): notes, tags, tasks, links and Bible references.

Long dropdowns (many tags, say) open a short list with a search box: type a few letters, then press Enter or click.

The app, updates and devices

  • Install Selah as an app: Settings → General → Install. On Android use Chrome. On iPhone, iPad and Mac use Safari. On a computer, Edge and Chrome both work.

  • Updates: when a new version is out, a banner says so. Click Reload; the note you're editing is saved first.

  • "Not secure" warning: Settings → Security → Trust this device gives the steps for each kind of device.

  • Light or dark: Settings → General → Appearance. Note text beside it reads notes in a serif (the default) or in the same sans as the rest of Selah.

The side panel

The panel button by Saved on an open note opens one panel beside the text, with tabs:

  • Outline: the note's headings; click one to go there.

  • Passage: the passages the note names (its title first), with the Bible text of the one you pick (the menu beside it switches translation), Insert as a quotation and Open in Scripture. Look up any other passage in the box at the top.

  • Sources (in a writing project) and Revise: see Writing.

Notes

My notes and tags

  • Tags sort your notes. A new note goes to My notes (made while a tag is open, it gets that tag). What you capture waits in the Inbox until you tag it; then it moves to My notes by itself.

  • Tags go in the box at the top of a note. The Tags page renames, merges, colors and deletes them, and Tag notes… tags many notes at once. Ignore Scripture references (⋯ or right-click a tag) keeps that tag's notes out of the Scripture index and Same Scripture connections; the tag shows No Scripture Indexing.

  • In the notes list, a note made by a builder has a small label saying which: Bible Study, Background, Word Study, Lesson, Sermon, or a piece of writing (Chapter, Blog Post, Paper, Devotional).

  • Untagged notes lists notes without a tag (Tags page, or right-click the Tags heading).

Writing in a note

  • Type / for the block menu: headings, lists, to-do lists, quotes, code, callouts, collapsible sections, tables, dividers, templates, citations, footnotes, Bible passages and more.

  • Markdown shortcuts work: # for a heading, - for a list, 1. for a numbered list, [ ] for a to-do, > for a quote.

  • Numbered lists number 1. → A. → I. → 1. → a. → i. → (1) → (a) → (i) as they nest deeper, the same in Preach mode, Word and PDF exports and on the iPad. Two lists of the same kind that end up touching (say, after you delete the line between them) join into one, so the numbers keep counting.

  • Moving list items: each item has its own grip, so drag one (its sub-items come with it) to another place in the list or out of it. Hold Alt as you start dragging any item to move the whole list. Alt+↑ / Alt+↓ move the item the cursor is in. On a phone, the List item row in the toolbar has Outdent, Indent, Move up and Move down.

  • Highlighter and text colors: the highlighter button uses the last color you picked, and the arrow beside it shows them all (yellow, green, blue, pink, orange, purple, or No highlight). The A button does the same for text color (red, orange, green, blue, purple, gray, or Default color). The colors adjust for dark mode, and go along to Word and WordPress.

  • Fold a heading: click the arrow beside a Heading 1 or 2 to hide everything under it.

  • Drag the handle at the left of a block to move it. Dragging a heading moves its whole section: a Heading 1 brings everything up to the next Heading 1, and a Heading 2 everything up to the next Heading 2 or 1.

  • Notes save as you type. The top bar says Saved.

  • Live updates: if the note you have open is changed in another window or on another device, the new text appears where you are (your cursor stays put) with a short notice. If you were in the middle of typing, a bar asks: Load theirs or Keep mine. Either way the other text is kept in Version history.

  • Full screen (the ⤢ button) hides the note list. A note you open from anywhere but the note list (Home, Ctrl+K, a link, a study) opens full screen; ⤢ brings the list back. Focus mode (the ✎ button, or Ctrl+Shift+F) shows only the text; Esc leaves it.

  • Find in note (Ctrl+F, or Find in note… in the ⋯ menu) highlights every match in the open note, even when it's read-only. Enter goes to the next, Shift+Enter the one before, and a match in a collapsed section opens it. Esc closes the bar and leaves the match selected. Select a word first and it's searched for.

  • The ⋯ menu lists Present, Preach or Teach and the builders only on sermons, lessons, Bible studies and notes in a teaching, preaching or writing study.

Read-only notes

  • The lock button by Saved switches a note to read-only, so you can read or scroll without changing anything by accident. Click it again to edit.

  • ⋯ → Always open read-only makes the note open locked every time (a lock shows on it in the note list). The lock button still unlocks it when you want to edit.

Pictures, files, drawings and recordings

  • Drop, paste or attach (📎) files. Pictures show in the note and can be resized (HEIC photos from a phone camera are shown as JPEG; Download keeps the original); PDFs show a preview in the note, and ⤢ opens them in a full-screen reader over the app (Esc or ✕ closes it); audio and video play in place.

  • /draw adds a drawing you can write in with a pen, mouse or finger.

  • /photo takes a picture with the camera. /record records audio.

Handwriting to text

  • /handwriting (Handwriting to text) types up written notes from a photo, a scan or a PDF. Choose a file or take a photo; each page is read, and you can fix the text before Insert into note. Tick Also add the photo to keep the original above the text.

  • In a drawing, To text types up what you wrote with the pen. Replace the drawing swaps the ink for the text; Insert below keeps both.

  • Pictures and PDFs already in a note: right-click a picture → Handwriting to text…, or use To text on a PDF.

  • Words it wasn't sure of are highlighted in the note so you can check them. Lines that start with a dash or bullet become a list, and 1. a numbered one.

  • It reads on Selah's own server, so nothing is sent anywhere else. Neat printing on flat, well-lit paper reads best; cursive is hard for it and needs more fixing.

Links between notes

  • Type [[ and part of a title, then pick the note. Create "…" makes a new note and links to it.

  • Links keep working when a note is renamed. Hover a link for a preview; right-click for more.

  • Connected notes, at the foot of every note, lists the notes that link here, notes that mention this note's title without a link (Link turns the mention into one), and notes on the same Scripture. It starts folded; click it to open. To see only the notes that link here, turn on Only notes I link in Settings → General.

Templates

  • Make into template (⋯ menu) turns a note into a template. Templates live in the Templates list.

  • Start a note from one with the template button next to New note, or insert one into the note you're writing with /template. In the picker, ↑ and ↓ choose a template and Enter uses it.

  • Placeholders fill in when used: {{date}}, {{time}}, {{weekday}} and {{year}}.

Cornell notes

  • Start one from the Cornell notes template (the template button next to New note), or type /cornell in any note.

  • The top holds the topic, date, course or study, and speaker. Below, write the notes on the right and the cues on the left: questions, key words and references to come back to. Finish with a summary at the bottom.

  • Tab moves from cue to note to the next row, and adds a new row at the end. From the header, Tab goes to the first cue; from the summary, out below the sheet.

  • To review, cover the right side and answer the cues from memory.

Version history

⋯ → Version history… shows the note as it was before each editing session, with what changed marked. Restore this version puts it back, and keeps what it replaced.

Pins, selecting several and the Trash

  • Pin to top of the list and Pin to Home are separate (⋯ or right-click).

  • Ctrl-click or Shift-click notes in the list to select several, then move, tag, pin or trash them together. On a phone, tap ✓ above the list first.

  • Deleted notes go to the Trash. Restore a note (or a group with Restore all). A note from a notebook that's gone (from before 10.1) comes back to My notes, tagged with the notebook's name. Keep deleted notes at the top of the Trash: until you empty it, or for 30, 60 or 90 days.

Sharing with others

In Selah in the cloud you can share a note, a whole notebook or a project on Desk with people who use Selah on the same server. They read it as you write it; only you can change it.

  • Contacts first. Utility → Shared with me → Contacts. Type someone’s Selah name (the first part of their web address) and send a request. Once they accept, you can share with them. Removing a contact ends everything shared between you, both ways.

  • Share. A note’s ⋯ → Share…; a notebook’s right-click menu → Share…; a study or course’s ⋯ on Desk → Share…. Tick the people. Allow comments is on unless you turn it off; Allow copying is off unless you turn it on. Untick everyone (or Stop sharing) to take it back; it goes from their Selah.

  • Live. A few seconds after you save, they see the change. A notebook’s new notes show up for them too. A note you share shows a Shared chip at the top.

  • Shared with you is in Utility, grouped by person, with a dot on what’s new or changed, and on Home. A notebook or project lists its notes on the left; lessons, sermons and Bible studies read as their pages.

  • Comments. On something shared with you, select words in the note and write in the Comments column. The owner sees the words highlighted in their note and the comment in the side panel’s Comments tab, can reply, and can Resolve it so it folds away. New comments and replies pop up in Selah and, in the Windows app or with Selah in the background, as a notification.

  • Make a copy (when the owner allows it) puts it in your own notes, where you can change it. A copy doesn’t follow the original any more.

Restore from a backup

For notes deleted forever, or as they were before a change: Settings → Data & backup → Restore from a backup… (or the link at the top of the Trash).

  • Pick a day. Selah keeps a copy of your notes each day for two weeks; with an off-site backup, its 30 days are listed too.

  • Look through its notebooks, or search. Each note says whether it was deleted since, is in the Trash, changed since or is the same as now; click one to read it as it was.

  • Tick whole notebooks or single notes and choose Restore. They come back as copies in My notes, with their tags and attachments, tagged with the notebook they were in: nothing you have now is changed. A note is marked “(restored Oct 20)” if one there already has that title.

Capture and the Inbox

Quick capture: Alt+N

  • Press Alt+N anywhere. The first line is the title. Enter saves; Shift+Enter starts a new line.

  • #tag tags it, !task makes it a task (with dates and priority read like Tasks quick-add).

  • Paste just a link to save a bookmark with the page's title and description.

  • Voice memo records audio to go with the note; Photo adds a picture and reads its words into the box (a page of a book, a whiteboard, a bulletin).

  • Switch Inbox to Sermon bank to save an illustration, quote, application or idea straight to the bank; #topic adds its topics. The bank keeps a photo’s words, not the photo.

  • In the phone and tablet app, + opens Quick capture (or Photo to text), Speak types what you say, Record adds a voice memo, Photo reads a picture’s words, and a long press on the Selah icon offers Quick capture, Voice memo, Photo to text and New task. With no connection, captures wait on the device (the offline page has a capture box too) and are sent when Selah can be reached; a photo’s words are read then.

Share from your phone

In the Selah app on Android, and with Selah installed from Chrome, Selah is in every app's share menu. Links (with the page's title), text, photos, PDFs and other files arrive in the Inbox. With no connection, what you share waits on the phone and goes to the Inbox once Selah can be reached. On iPhone and iPad, Selah joins the share sheet in a later app update.

The Edge clipper

  • Set it up in Settings → Connections → Set up clipper…: download it, load it in Edge with Developer mode, and connect it with the one-time code.

  • Clip a page as an Article, a Selection, a Bookmark, or a screenshot of the Screen or an Area. Add tags (a tagged clip goes straight to My notes), or add it to an existing note.

  • Right-click a page, selection, link or picture to save it, or press Alt+Shift+S.

  • The Sermon bank tab puts the text you selected into the sermon bank, with a link to the page. Pick its kind, a title, topics and a passage (left empty, the first Scripture reference in the text fills it). Pick a sermon under For a sermon and it comes first in that sermon's bank on the Arrange board. The clipper remembers which tab and kind you used last.

Filing the Inbox

Filing is tagging: select an item and press 1–6 to tag it with a tag on the bar (tags named in it come first), T to type any tag, or Delete to trash it. A tagged item moves to My notes. Every action has Undo. ↑ and ↓ move; Enter opens.

Home

Your desk at a glance: where Selah opens, and the rail's Home. Its tabs are Home, then Study, Teach, Write and Learn, each with how much is active. Those four list only the work that's still active: each study, lesson, sermon and plan with the step it's on (Step 3 of 6) and Mark done, which takes it off Home (it stays on Desk under Done, and Undo puts it back). Learn starts with This week: what's due and the lectures coming up. The Home tab has two columns: the reading, next up and this week on the left; what to pick up, pinned notes and the status line on the right.

  • Daily Walk: today's reading, with Read and Journal. Once you've read it, it shrinks to a small check.

  • Verse of the Day: Bible Gateway's verse for today (when it can't be reached, the card says so), read in your favorite translation (the menu on the card switches it). Hover the reference for its card. Study it starts a Bible study of it, New note puts it in a note in your Inbox, and the copy button copies it to paste into any note.

  • Next up: from Desk, the next lesson or sermon you teach and how ready it is (the four bars are Hook, Book, Look and Took), with Open (its page) and Teach or Preach; your writing projects' words toward their goals, your studies' pace, and what's due in your classes.

  • This week: the next seven days in one list: your events, your tasks (tick one to finish it) and Desk's dates (teaching days, readings, milestones, due dates), with anything overdue first. Add a task in the box at the top. The funnel chooses which calendar categories show.

  • Pick up: the notes you were last in, from your studies and everywhere else. Pinned notes (⋯ or right-click → Pin to Home) show under them.

  • A line at the bottom says how much space you're using and when you were last backed up, in color when something needs a look; click it for Settings.

  • Customize (the button beside New) shows or hides each part. New makes a note, task or event.

Plan: calendar and tasks

Calendar

  • Plan on the rail is the calendar with your tasks beside it: what's overdue, today's tasks and the ones with no date, with an add box and a list filter. Drag a task onto a day to give it that date (or onto a time in the week and day views). All tasks opens the full Tasks page; its Plan button comes back.

  • Month, week, day and agenda views. Click or drag to make an event; drag to move or resize it.

  • Events can repeat (daily, chosen weekdays, monthly, yearly) and link to a note.

  • List shows every event in a period, grouped by day.

Attendance

Plan's Attendance tab keeps track of who comes: the classes you teach, the services you preach and your small groups.

  • New group: pick Class, Service or Small group, the repeating calendar event it meets as (its meetings then show up on their own), and whether you check off names or just count heads. Paste a roster, one name per line, or add people later.

  • A group shows its meetings as a strip of dates, today's (or the last one) open. Tap who's here in the grid of names (sorted by last name, so couples sit together), use the filter box to find someone, or tap Everyone's here. Add a visitor by name at the meeting they came to, and count others not on the roster. A service just takes a headcount. Each meeting can have notes; Clear takes it back.

  • Follow up: when someone on a roster misses three in a row, they show at the top. Follow-up task adds "Follow up with …" to today's tasks; Dismiss if you're already in touch. Either way they don't come back until they're there again and then miss three more.

  • Manage people, under the names: each with how many they've come to. Rename, make a visitor a regular, take someone off the roster (their attendance stays) or delete them.

  • CSV downloads the group's attendance for a spreadsheet: a row per person, a column per meeting (P or A), with the totals. The ⋯ menu edits, archives or deletes a group, or takes attendance for another day.

Categories

  • Each event has a category (Church, Family, School…) instead of a color. The category gives it its color and an optional icon, shown on the calendar, the list and Home. Pick it in the event's Category row, where New… adds one on the spot.

  • The chips above the calendar show or hide each category (and events with none); this device remembers your choice. Home's This week has its own filter (the funnel button).

  • Manage… (beside the chips): rename, recolor, choose an icon, reorder, delete (its events stay, without a category), and pick the default category for new events. A category can also give its events reminders (say, Church: 1 day before); an event that sets its own uses those instead.

  • A class's assignments (Desk → Learn → Due dates from Canvas → On the calendar as) show in a category's color and icon, and hide with it.

  • When categories arrived (5.7), each event color you used became a category named after it, ready to rename.

Tasks

  • Views: Today, Upcoming, All tasks, Inbox (no list) and Completed, plus your own lists.

  • Quick add understands plain words: "Call Bob tomorrow 3pm !high #Church" or "Water plants every Mon and Thu". The ? button lists what it reads.

  • Click a task for its details: steps (a checklist), notes, a linked note, start and due dates, repeat.

  • Drag tasks to reorder them, onto a day to reschedule, or onto a list. Select several to change them together.

  • / searches; the filter and sort buttons narrow and order the list. N jumps to the add box.

  • A task with a start date stays out of Today until it starts.

  • Remind me (on a task with a due date, and on every event): any of at the time, 15 minutes, 1 hour, 12 hours, 1 day or 1 week before, or None. Usually an event reminds you 15 minutes before and a task at its time; all-day events and tasks without a time have none unless you pick some, and those count back from midnight. Reminders need to be turned on for the device (Settings → General → Notifications). In the phone and tablet app on Selah Cloud, a reminder you add or change on another device while the app is closed still comes, and a share from someone arrives as a notification.

Daily Walk, Reading Plans and Prayer

Reading Plans

Walk → Plans reads through the Bible with a plan. Today's passages also show on Home with a tick apiece.

  • New plan offers three: Bible in a Year (some Old Testament, some New Testament, and a Psalm or Proverb each day), M’Cheyne (his 1842 calendar, four readings a day) and Chronological (the Bible in the order its events happened; scholars differ on some placements). Bible in a Year and M’Cheyne can follow the calendar, so today is today's reading and earlier days don't count as missed, or start at day 1.

  • Build your own: pick books (or Whole Bible, Old or New Testament, Gospels, Psalms & Proverbs, Paul's letters), then how fast: a number of reading days, chapters a day, or a date to finish by. Choose the start and which days of the week you read; the preview says when you'll finish.

  • Import a CSV: one row per day, the day number first, then its passages in the next columns (or several in one, separated by “;”). Download the template to start from. Rows with a problem are listed before anything is saved.

  • Each passage opens to its text in your translation (the switcher at the top). Tick passages as you read, or Mark all read.

  • Behind? A plan says how many days. Catch up lists the missed days to read and tick; Shift dates makes today the first day you missed, and the rest of the plan moves later.

  • A plan's ⋯: See the whole plan (every day with its date; tick any of them), Rename, Pause, Download as CSV (edit it in a spreadsheet and import it again), Start over and Delete.

  • Journal opens the day's journal note (the same one as Daily Walk); it lists each plan's passages for the day.

Daily Walk

  • Your routine for the day, as cards in the order you set (Edit routine): your prayer list, each devotional's reading in full, a reading plan's passages in full, and anything of your own (a memory verse, quiet time) with its note, link or timer. Tick each as you go; a day with everything done counts toward your streak.

  • A devotional is an EPUB of your own (Edit routine → Devotional (EPUB)…). Only DRM-free EPUBs can be added: books from Kindle, Apple Books and most stores are copy-protected. A second devotional is a second card.

  • Books headed by date (“October 19”) are read on that date. Books headed by day (“Day 1”) follow their length: 365 or 366 days follow the calendar (Feb 29 only in leap years), 260 days follow the year's weekdays, and any other length asks whether to read every day or on weekdays, starting the day you add it. Schedule changes that later.

  • Move between days with the week strip or ← and →; T goes to today.

  • Tick each reading when you've read it. The stats show your streak and history.

  • Journal (at the top) adds a line or two to the day's journal note, or opens the note.

Prayer

  • Add requests with who they're for, a category, verses and details. Quick add reads @Person #Category mon,thu and references.

  • Each request has a rhythm: every day, chosen weekdays, or in rotation (a few each day).

  • Today lists what's due. Mark requests answered, with how; the Answered tab keeps them as a timeline.

  • Add to prayer list… in a note's ⋯ menu makes a request from the selected text.

Scripture

References in your notes

  • Bible references are underlined. Hover one for its text in your favorite translation (and your verse notes on those verses). With more than one translation connected, the buttons at the bottom of the card switch it, and the card remembers your pick.

  • Ctrl+click opens it in Logos. Right-click for Open in Logos, Find notes on this passage, Insert text in your favorite translation (as a quote, or inline right after the reference) and Copy Logos link.

  • /bible (or /esv) inserts a passage: pick the translation, then Quote (a block quotation with verse numbers) or Inline (“For God so loved the world…” (John 3:16 ESV), inside your sentence). The quote ends with the translation’s name. It remembers your last choices. /logos adds a link to a book in Logos.

  • Paste a link or a quote copied from Logos: links become readable, and a quote's citation becomes a footnote.

Reading a passage

  • On the Scripture page, type a reference and press Go. Just those verses show, one per row. Buttons move to the chapter before or after, or show the Whole chapter.

  • Beside the text is everything you've written on the passage. First, Your work on the passage: your Bible studies, word studies, lessons and sermons on those verses, each with its step (or Done), a study's keeper and a sermon's preaching dates; click one to open its builder, or Read the note. Study this passage and Study a word start new ones. Then the book's background, sessions you've planned but not written (Write the lesson), its memory verses, and other notes that mention it. Click one to open it in a panel over the page.

  • Verse notes: click a verse (or its +) to jot a note on it. Ctrl+Enter saves. It's a regular note titled with the verse (in the Bible Study notebook), so search, tags and links all work, and it shows beside its verse here. Any note titled with one to three verses shows the same way. (Margin notes from before 5.10 became verse notes.)

  • Photo of your handwritten notes: take a picture of your Bible's margins or a notebook page. The text is read and saved as a note on the passage.

  • Typing words instead of a reference searches your notes.

Bible studies

A guided study of a passage through the Interpretive Journey from J. Scott Duvall and J. Daniel Hays's Grasping God's Word, with the fields of your Scripture Study Template. Start one with New study: on Home (New → Study…), Desk's Study tab, the Bible Studies tab, Ctrl+K, or Study this passage on a passage. Type a passage, a book or a word, and the kind follows what you type (a verse or chapter: a passage study; a book on its own: its background; a Strong's number, Greek, Hebrew or another word: a word study); click another kind to change it. File under puts it in a study plan as one of its readings (the reading already planned for that passage, if there is one); the builder opens at once, and a note at the bottom says where the study lives. Above a study in its note, and in its builder, a path shows the same: Bible studies › your plan › the study. Study it on a reading in a study plan works as before (see Desk). A study note shows a Bible study chip, and Study builder… in its ⋯ menu. Sermons, lessons and writing chapters keep their own workflows and can't become studies: a study is the first step, and it feeds them.

  • Passage and Reading: count each time you read it through (Read it again) and the translations you consulted.

  • 1. The Text in Their Town: people, places and things; important words; questions; and the text summary, what it meant to them (past tense).

Word studies

  • The lesson builder walks through Lawrence Richards's Hook, Book, Look, Took (Creative Bible Teaching), with the passage beside you and each step's key question opening to guidance. Courses

Sermons

The Sermons page (under Desk) keeps every sermon: built here, or brought in from Logos. A sermon is an ordinary note laid out the way Logos's Sermon Builder and your Sermon Preparation Template lay it out: the passage, Introduction (Attention, Need, Bible Text, Bridging Sentences), one Heading 1 per division with its transition, and Conclusion (Visualization, Reiteration, Action).

The sermon builder

  • New sermon asks for a title, passage and series, then opens the builder. A sermon note shows a Sermon chip that opens it, and Sermon builder… in its ⋯ menu.

  • Its twelve steps: text structure, text details and research questions (a worksheet kept with the sermon, not in the note); the text idea (subject and modifier); the bridge (sermon idea, interrogative, transition); the divisions; their explanation, illustration, argumentation and application (with a bar showing the balance); analogies; pictures and stories; human experience; the faith response; and the oral presentation (delivery notes and the length at about 130 words a minute).

  • Save to note (or Ctrl+S) writes it into the note. Only the parts you changed are rewritten: everything else in the note, lists and links included, stays as it was, and the note's version history keeps the version before. Parts with lists or pictures show as a preview to edit in the note.

  • The transition's promise ("for three reasons") is checked against the number of divisions.

Credit: the builder's twelve steps are based on Wayne McDill's 12 Essential Skills for Great Preaching (2nd ed., B&H Academic, 2006). The book teaches the full method; the builder walks you through it.

Arranging a sermon

  • Arrange (at the top of the builder) shows the whole sermon as cards: the Introduction, each division, the Conclusion, their parts (Explanation, Illustration…), and every paragraph and list item. Drag a card by its ⠿ grip, or use its ⋯ menu (Move up, Move down, Move to…, Indent, Outdent) on a phone or for a long move.

  • To take something out, choose Remove in a card's ⋯ menu (a paragraph, a list item with its sub-points, a part, or a whole division), or drag the card onto Drop here to remove, which appears at the top while you drag. Undo brings it back; nothing leaves the note until you save.

  • Move whole divisions, move a part from one division to another (an Illustration dropped where there is one already joins it), or move single paragraphs and sub-points. The dashed + Illustration-style slots start a part a division doesn't have yet.

  • After a move the divisions renumber, and a Reiteration filled in from the divisions is listed again in the new order (one you wrote yourself gets a warning instead). A transition that now leads into a different division, or is on the last one, is flagged: Rewrite it or say It still fits. Flags stay until you deal with them, even after saving.

  • Undo (or Ctrl+Z) takes back each move. Compare shows the sermon as last saved beside how it is now. Outline shows each paragraph's first line; Full Text shows everything. Nothing reaches the note until you save.

Delivery order

  • The builder keeps each division grouped by element (Explanation, Illustration, Argumentation, Application), but you may preach them interleaved. On the Arrange board, each paragraph or list item under a division has a # box: type 1, 2, 3… for the order you'll preach them (Enter or click away to set it; empty clears it). Sub-points go with the item above them. A number used twice turns orange.

  • A division's ⋯ menu has Number in the order shown (a quick start you can then change) and Clear the numbers.

  • Delivery Order (beside Outline and Full Text) shows each division as it will be preached, each block marked with its element. What sits right under the division heading stays first unless you number it; other blocks without a number come after the numbered ones. A division with no numbers is preached as grouped.

  • The note itself stays grouped, so the steps and the board work as before; the numbers stay on the paragraphs even when you retype them in the steps or edit the note. Preaching mode and Export to Word use the delivery order (Word has a checkbox to turn it off).

  • Color-coding (optional): the highlighter button in preaching mode, or Color-code the elements when exporting a sermon to Word, marks each element with its own color and name: blue Explanation, amber Illustration, purple Argumentation and green Application.

The sermon bank

  • The bank (beside the board) keeps reusable illustrations, quotes, Scripture and applications for any sermon. What you write in the builder stays with that sermon; only what you add to the bank is shared.

  • Add to it with New, with Save to the sermon bank… on any card, or by selecting text in any note, right-clicking it and choosing Add to sermon bank… (Shift+right-click gives the browser's own menu). Items keep a link back to the note they came from.

  • The Edge clipper's Sermon bank tab adds text selected on a web page, with a link to the page. Items clipped for a sermon come first in its bank, marked Clipped for this sermon.

  • Drag an item into the sermon to use a copy of it; the bank remembers which sermons used it. Search it, or filter it by kind; items on the sermon's book come first.

Preaching mode

  • Preach (▶ on the Sermons page, in the builder, or a sermon's ⋯ menu) shows the note read-only in large type, light or dark, and keeps the screen awake.

  • Manuscript and Outline both show everything in full. They differ only for divisions with delivery numbers (below).

  • A sermon with numbered blocks reads in its delivery order. In Manuscript each block is a paragraph; in Outline each numbered division becomes a numbered list: every block a main point (1, 2, 3…), its further lines and sub-points beneath it (A, B…). The highlighter button color-codes Explanation, Illustration, Argumentation and Application.

  • Tap a heading to fold it; the outline button jumps to a section or folds everything. Tap a Bible reference to show its text in place.

  • The timer (tap it, or T to start and pause) counts down from your goal time: tap the words beside it to set one (it starts from the sermon's planned minutes, and setting it here updates them).   • A sermon with slides gets the slide bar along the bottom; see Slides below.

Slides (lessons and sermons)

  • Every lesson and sermon can have a slide deck of its own. Open it from the Slides tab at the top of the sermon or lesson builder, or Slides… in the note's ⋯ menu. A new deck is built from the outline: for a lesson the title, big idea, passage, each outline point (its sub-points come in one at a time), each discussion question (lines under a question become its answer) and the Took; for a sermon the title, passage, sermon idea, each division with its verses, and a summary.

  • Slides come in six types: Title, Point, Passage (the passage in your favorite translation, one verse a page, a long verse carried on to the next page at a sentence or clause break, so it stays large), Image (a picture from the note or a new upload), Question (its answer shows only on your screen) and Custom. Text takes **bold**, *italics* and "- " bullets. Add slide puts one after the slide you're on; drag slides in the list to reorder them, or right-click one. Changes save by themselves.

  • Canvas shows the slides down the left, the one you're on large in the middle (↑ and ↓ move between them), and a panel on the right. Slide holds its words, picture, passage, where it comes up and your notes; under This slide's background it can have its own art, a picture behind it, or no logo. Outline lists every slide's title and text to type straight through; click a slide's picture to open it in the canvas.

  • Design: the deck's theme (Lamplight, Lamplight Dark, Parchment, Linen, Dawn, Forest, Slate, Midnight, or your church brand), its colors (the theme's, another theme's, the church brand's, or your own background, text and accent), its fonts (Classic, Book, Elegant, Modern, Bold or Clean; the theme picks one), its background art (line drawings such as a cross, dove, olive branch, lamp or wheat, set faintly beside the text, or textures such as parchment, linen and watercolor; all in the theme's colors), a picture behind every slide (darkened as much as you like so the words stay readable), and the translation its passages read in (your favorite unless you pick another; a passage slide can pick its own).

  • Church brand (Design → Edit the church brand, or Utility → Media): your church's name, three colors, a light, dark or colored background, fonts, background art and logo (in the corner you choose). Any deck can then use it as its theme; the logo shows on every slide unless you turn it off for the deck or a slide.

  • Utility → Media keeps the church brand, My pictures (pictures for slides and backgrounds, kept in Selah rather than in a note) and the built-in background art, which you can see in any theme.

  • Download → Pictures saves every slide as a picture (PNG, 1920 × 1080) exactly as it shows when presenting, in a .zip, for other presentation software or a screen that only takes pictures.

  • Download → PowerPoint downloads the deck as a .pptx: each slide in its theme, fonts, art, picture and logo, with the words as text you can still edit; a long passage takes as many slides as it has pages, and your notes and a question's answer go in the speaker notes. PowerPoint shows all of a slide's points at once.

  • Comes up when I reach ties a slide to a place in the note (a heading, list item or paragraph). Notes for you show on your screen while presenting, never on the slide.

  • When the note's outline changes, nothing changes on its own: slides it touches say Note changed (with Update from the note or Keep mine), slides whose part is gone say Not in the note, and new points are offered at the top (Add them, or Not these).

  • The slides window is what the room sees. Open it from the Slides tab, the presenter view or preaching mode, drag it to the projector or TV, and press F for full screen (it opens where you left it next time; where the browser can tell, On the other screen puts it there for you). A clicker or the arrow keys work in it too. It follows through the server as well, so it keeps up with the phone remote and works in a different browser from the one you present in, or on another computer at the projector: open /#slides there, signed in, and it shows whatever you're presenting.

  • Preaching with slides: preaching mode gets a slide bar along the bottom. With Follow my place on, each slide comes up as its place in the note reaches the top third of the screen; numbered pins beside the text put a slide up straight away, and ◀ ▶ move them by hand. Blank blanks the room's screen.

  • Phone remote: tap Phone remote in the slide bar or the presenter view and scan the code with your phone (or open /#remote on it, signed in to Selah). It shows the slide on show and the next, the timer (and the lesson step), a question's answer and your notes, with big Back and Next buttons and Blank. It follows whatever you're preaching, teaching or presenting, and keeps the phone awake.

  • Present on a lesson or sermon opens the presenter view: the slide on show and the next one, the question's answer and your notes, a timer, and every slide along the bottom to jump to. With only one screen, Present here shows the slides full screen (Esc comes back).

Library, log and series

  • Library: search, and filter by status (building, ready, preached, never preached) or book. Right-click a sermon for more.

  • Preached: where and when each was preached, by year, filterable by place.

  • Series: sermon series are studies. Plan the series… dates the sermons; Build sermon on a session opens the builder, and logging a sermon ticks its session.

  • Import from Logos…: in Sermon Builder, export each sermon as Rich Text (.rtf) and choose the files. Title, passage, series and topics come across (topics as tags, a series as a sermon series), with headings, lists and Logos links; pictures don't.

Writing

Citations and footnotes

  • Connect Zotero in Settings → Connections. Then /cite, the toolbar's cite button, or Ctrl+Alt+F cites a source with a page. Click a citation to change it.

  • /footnote adds a footnote in your own words.

  • Each note shows its Notes and Bibliography under the text, in your citation style (Turabian by default; change it in Settings → Connections).

Writing projects

  • A Writing project (a kind of study) holds chapters: each is its own note. Put them in order (drag or arrows).

  • Project type (in Edit…): Book, Blog Post or Series, Research Paper (Turabian) or Devotional or Article. It names the pieces (chapters, posts, parts) and lays out each new one: a post starts with Hook, Body and Takeaway, a devotional with Scripture, Reflection, Application and Prayer, a paper with Introduction, Conclusion and Bibliography; a book's chapter starts blank. A project without a type asks when you add a piece (tick Use it for every piece to set it), and Switch method… in a piece's ⋯ menu lays it out in another type. A paper started from a class assignment is a Research Paper.

  • A book has Front matter (Preface, Introduction…) and Back matter (Appendix, Bibliography…): they stay before and after the chapters, marked F and B instead of numbered.

  • Stages: each piece moves through Idea, Research, Outline, Draft, Revise, Edit and Final. Each stage has a short checklist about the work (the 0/2 button). List or Board shows the pieces in order or by stage; drag a card to another stage. Desk → Write → Board shows every project's pieces together.

  • Word counts show per chapter and in total. Give the project a word goal in Edit…, and a chapter one from its ⋯ menu. The project shows words written each day.

  • Reading notes go in the project's Notes box.

  • While writing a chapter, the Sources button (by Saved) opens a panel with the project's reading notes and the sources it cites; click a source to cite it at the cursor.

Revise

The marker button by Saved on a chapter or post (or Revise… in any note's ⋯ menu) opens a panel beside the text. It runs simple rules in your browser, with no AI and nothing sent anywhere. It points at places worth a second look and says why; it never suggests wording, so the ideas and the words stay yours. Quotations, block quotes and citations are never checked.

  • Overview: words, reading time, sentences, average sentence length, reading level (the Flesch–Kincaid grade: words per sentence and syllables per word), and words added and taken out since the last draft you marked.

  • Highlights: turn on long sentences, passive voice, -ly adverbs, filler words, hedges, repeated words and your own words to watch (Settings in the panel, with the long-sentence length). Each is marked in its own color; click one to see why, step through them with the arrows, or Dismiss it (dismissals are kept for the note). A research paper also gets Turabian checks: a quotation with no footnote, a claim that reads like a source with no footnote, a long quotation that should be a block quotation, and contractions.

  • Cut: choose 5–25% and watch the words left to cut; the paragraphs with the most marked per hundred words are listed first. What to cut is yours to decide.

  • Drafts: Mark this draft… names a copy in the note's version history that's kept for good; compare any two drafts (or a draft and now), with words added and taken out.

  • Structure: the question each part of the project type answers, and how much you've written there.

  • Read aloud reads from the cursor in your browser's voice, highlighting each sentence.

Export

Export… is in a note's ⋯ and right-click menus, a tag's menu, the note list's selection menu, and Settings → Data & backup → Export everything…. The dialog says how many notes and attachments go. Formats:

  • Word (a note, selected notes or one notebook): one document, each note starting on a new page, in the order you choose (oldest first, A–Z or newest first). See below.

  • Markdown: a .md file per note, starting with its title, tags, dates and notebook, with its attachments in an attachments folder beside it. A note with no files downloads as one .md file; more come as a zip, in shelf and notebook folders.

  • Web pages: a page per note and a contents page (index.html) that open in any browser, in light or dark.

  • .enex file: an .enex file per notebook (a zip of them for several), attachments inside. Apps that import .enex files read it, and so does Selah's own import.

  • Selah notebook: one .selahnb file with the notes exactly as they are, their tags and attachments. Settings → Data & backup → Import → Selah notebook brings it into any Selah as new notebooks, so it's a way to share a study series or keep a notebook on its own.

Along the way: citations and your footnotes become numbered footnotes, drawings become pictures (SVG), to-do lists keep their checkmarks, callouts become Markdown callouts, iPhone photos (HEIC) go as JPEG, and a link to another note goes to that note's file when it's in the export too (otherwise it's plain text).

Merge and split notes

  • Merge: select notes in the list (Ctrl+click, or the ✓ select button), then ⋯ or right-click → Merge … into one…. Put them in order (drag, or the arrows), give it a title, and choose whether each note starts with its title as a heading or a divider. Tags and attachments come along.

  • Split: a note's ⋯ menu → Split into notes…. Start a new note at each Heading 1, 2 or 3, or each divider; the preview lists the notes it'll make. Each takes its heading as its title (text before the first one keeps the note's title). Tick Tag them … to tag the parts with the note's title so they stay together, and add an optional contents note linking them in order.

  • Either way the original notes go to the Trash (or tick Keep), and links to them point at the new note (or the contents note). Attachments are copied, so a note restored from the Trash still has its files.

Word formats

A note's or notebook's Export… → Word, or Export to Word… on a writing project for every chapter at once. Pick a format each time:

  • Turabian paper: double-spaced Times New Roman, footnotes, bibliography, page numbers, and a title page if you want one.

  • Manuscript: clean and single-spaced, for sermons and lessons.

  • Export the manuscript (the download button in teach and preaching mode, or the lesson and sermon builders’ last step) saves a lesson or sermon as a PDF, a Word file or rich text (.rtf), in Regular, Large or Pulpit print. A sermon can go out as a manuscript or an outline, in delivery order and color-coded.

  • Export for iPad (the tablet button in teach and preaching mode, or the lesson and sermon builders’ last step) makes a .selahlesson file for Selah Companion, the iPad app for preaching and teaching from your lessons and sermons. It carries the text as preaching mode shows it (a sermon in delivery order, its elements color-coded), its slides, and either the Bible text of its references or just the references. Put the file in iCloud Drive, OneDrive or Google Drive and open it on the iPad. Export it again after a change and the iPad updates the same lesson, keeping the notes you wrote there. In Selah Desktop it can save straight into a folder the iPad opens (Settings → Data & backup → Selah Companion).

  • Plain: the note as written.

If the document quotes the Bible, it ends with the copyright notice for each translation it quotes, as the publishers ask. WordPress posts do the same.

WordPress

  • Connect your site in Settings → Connections with an application password.

  • Post to WordPress… (⋯ menu) fills in categories, tags, excerpt, featured picture and Scripture references. Save as a draft, publish now, or schedule it. Posting again updates the same post.

  • Devotionals: with Scripture Blog 2.7.0 or later, choose Post as: Devotional. Pick the Day it's for and the Verse (Fill in the verse looks it up in your favorite translation). A note written with the Devotional method fills these in: its Scripture section gives the verse and its Application section fills the site's Application box. A published devotional appears on its day; one shows per day.

  • Series: in a writing project, turn on Post the chapters as a series. Each posted chapter gets the series name, "Part 2 of 5", and links to the parts before and after.

Attachments

Focus mode

  • For study and writing: Ctrl+Shift+F or the focus button on the top bar turns it on and off. While it's on, the button is filled in.

  • Turning it on keeps you on Daily Walk, Prayer, Desk, Sermons or Scripture; from any other page it goes to Daily Walk. Turning it off goes to Home.

Settings, backup and security

  • General: appearance, sidebar, Focus mode, Notifications (reminders on this device for events and tasks; each one sets its own under Remind me), Connected notes (Only notes I link) and installing the app. In the Selah app on a phone or tablet, the lock asks for Face ID, a fingerprint or the device's passcode when Selah opens and after 5 minutes away. On Windows, a Mac or Linux you can also Download Selah Desktop: its own window and a tray (menu-bar on a Mac) icon (closing the window keeps it running so reminders still arrive). When it opens, choose On a Selah Cloud server and type this Selah's address to use it there, or On this computer for a library of your own (see Selah Desktop below).

  • Quick capture (the tray or menu-bar menu, or Ctrl+Alt+N from anywhere; Cmd+Option+N on a Mac) opens a small window on top of whatever you're doing. Under Note, type a thought and it goes to your Inbox (with no title, the first line is the title). Under Sermon bank, add a quote, illustration or the like with its kind, passage and topics, for any sermon or one in progress. Ctrl+Enter saves and puts the window away; Esc puts it away. If another program already uses Ctrl+Alt+N, the shortcut isn't shown in the menu and Quick capture is there instead.

  • Connections: the Edge clipper, WordPress, Zotero and citation style, Logos, and Bible. Bible connects translations with your own free keys: the ESV (api.esv.org), the NLT (api.nlt.to) and API.Bible (api.bible: tick the Bibles your key may use, such as the KJV or WEB; the free plan allows 5,000 lookups a month, and stored text is looked up again after 30 days). Pick your Favorite translation: hover cards, quotations, preaching pop-ins and slides use it, and the side panel, the builders' passage pane and the Scripture page each have their own menu to switch. The NLT and API.Bible dialogs show their lookups and stored verses. The ESV's dialog shows how many lookups the ESV has answered against Crossway's limits (60 a minute, 1,000 an hour, 5,000 a day), how many were read free from the stored verses, and how many verses are stored (500 at most); a message appears once you pass 80% of the hourly or daily limit. Show stored passages lists them, most recently read first; forget one (or all) and it's looked up again next time.

  • Credits: everything Selah uses from others and on what terms: Bible text and services, the methods the builders follow and their authors, data, fonts and open-source software. Selah is free software under the GNU AGPL; About → Source code links to it.

  • Data & backup: on your own server the encrypted off-site backup, in Selah Desktop automatic backups; downloading a backup, restoring notes from a backup, and importing .enex files, Markdown or a Selah notebook file.

    • Off-site backup sends an encrypted copy every night to storage you own. Choose where:

      • OneDrive: Connect OneDrive opens Microsoft's sign-in in a new tab. Afterwards that tab says it can't reach the page, which is expected: copy its whole address (it starts with http://localhost:53682/) and paste it back.

      • S3-compatible (Backblaze B2, Wasabi, Cloudflare R2, Amazon S3 or another): make a private bucket and a key that can reach only that bucket, then enter the bucket, the key and (for some services) the endpoint or region. The dialog says where each service shows them.

      • WebDAV (Nextcloud, Synology, Koofr…): the server's WebDAV address, your user name and an app password.

      Test connection checks the storage takes a file before you go on. Then choose a passphrase, keep it somewhere safe, and use Test passphrase… now and then. Change… signs in again or moves the backup somewhere else.

    • Download a backup makes a fresh copy of everything as one file, to keep wherever you like (iCloud Drive, another cloud, a USB stick). Encrypted with a passphrase (recommended) gives a .selahbak file nobody can open without it; Not encrypted gives a .tar.gz any zip tool opens. Either one restores a Selah: on your own server with npm run restore -- --file, or ask your host.

  • Account: the username and email you sign in with (Change…; it asks for your password first if you haven't typed it lately), how much storage you're using, and Download all my data (the same download as Data & backup → Download a backup). On a Selah hosted for you, it also shows your host's nightly backup and has Delete account…, which deletes your Selah, everything in it and your host's backups of it.

  • Security: the panel at the top says whether two-step sign-in is on, when you last signed in and from where, and any failed attempts in the last day.

    • Two-step sign-in (recommended): after your password, Selah asks for a 6-digit code from an authenticator app on your phone (Microsoft Authenticator, Google Authenticator, 1Password, Authy and others). Turn on… shows a QR code to scan; then save the ten recovery codes somewhere apart from your phone. Each one signs you in once if the phone is lost (Lost your phone? Use a recovery code on the sign-in page). New recovery codes… replaces them. Turning it on signs out your other devices. Until it's on, Home shows a reminder (✕ puts it away); Turn off goes back to the password alone.

    • Confirm it's you: changes to how you sign in (two-step sign-in, recovery codes, connecting the Edge clipper) ask for your password, and a code, again unless you entered them in the last 10 minutes.

    • Password: at least 12 characters, and not one known from a data breach (only the first few characters of a scrambled copy are checked online; the password never leaves the server). Changing it signs out other devices.

    • Signed-in devices: each browser, phone or Windows app that's signed in, with when it was last used and its address, plus your Edge clippers. Sign one out, or Sign out all other devices.

    • Sign out when idle: a device that hasn't opened Selah for 1 day, 1 week, 2 weeks (the default) or 30 days has to sign in again. A sign-in lasts 30 days at most either way.

    • Activity: sign-ins, wrong passwords and codes, and security changes, with the device and address. After you sign in, Selah tells you if there were failed attempts since last time.

    • After 10 failed attempts from one address (or 50 in all) in 15 minutes, signing in pauses for 15 minutes. Trust this device fixes "Not secure" warnings (on your own server).

  • About: the version, What's new (a few lines for each update; a card shows them once after an update), this guide, and on your own server the Maintenance guide (looking after the server: the selah command, starting at boot, updates, backups and fixes) and the Full changelog.

A Selah hosted for you

  • Selah can be hosted for several people, each with their own Selah at their own address and their notes kept completely apart. Your host sends you a setup link: open it, choose a username, your email and a password, then scan the QR code with an authenticator app and save your recovery codes (recommended; Skip for now leaves two-step sign-in for Settings → Security). The link works once, and nobody else ever knows your password.

  • The sign-in page asks for your username or email (either one) and your password. A Selah set up before usernames asks you to choose one, and an email, the first time you sign in; until you do, the password alone signs in.

  • Two-step sign-in is recommended but up to you. To use a new phone, Move to a new phone… in Settings → Security sets it up there.

  • Forgot your password, or lost your phone and your recovery codes? Ask your host for a reset link: it lets you choose a new password and set up your authenticator again (or skip it, which turns the old one off). Your notes aren't touched.

  • Your host backs everything up every night. You can keep your own encrypted copy as well: Download a backup whenever you like (Settings → Data & backup). Your host can restore your Selah from it, and Selah Desktop can open it as a library of its own.

Selah Desktop

  • Selah Desktop is Selah as a program for Windows, Mac or Linux. With On this computer your library lives on the computer: it works without the internet (Bible text, WordPress and Logos links still need it), there's no sign-in, and Settings has no Account or Security tabs. With On a Selah Cloud server it shows your Selah from the server, signed in as in a browser. Settings → General → App switches between them.

  • The tray (the menu bar on a Mac): closing the window keeps Selah running, so reminders still arrive. Click its icon for Quick capture (also Ctrl+Alt+N anywhere, Cmd+Option+N on a Mac), Start with Windows (Open at login on a Mac or Linux), Check for updates and Quit Selah. On a Mac, click Selah in the Dock to bring the window back. New versions download by themselves; Selah asks before restarting.

  • Automatic backups (Settings → Data & backup): choose a folder, ideally in OneDrive, iCloud Drive, Dropbox or Google Drive so a copy leaves the computer (Selah suggests one). Daily or weekly, the newest ten kept, optionally encrypted with a passphrase. Back up now makes one straight away; Home shows when the last one was made. Go back to a backup replaces the whole library with one (yours is kept aside), and Restore notes brings back just some notes, as copies, from any of them.

  • Moving a library: Import a library opens a backup file from another computer (Windows, Mac or Linux) or from Selah Cloud (it replaces the library on this computer). To move to Selah Cloud, Download a backup and have it restored there.

  • Lock (Settings → General → App): a PIN for Selah, and Windows Hello or Touch ID where the computer has them, asked when Selah opens and after you've been away (5 minutes to 4 hours). It keeps someone at your computer out of your notes.

  • Slides on a second screen: with a projector or TV connected, the slides window opens full screen on it and your controls stay on the laptop. The phone remote needs a Selah Cloud server.
